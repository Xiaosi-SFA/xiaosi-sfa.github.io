On the evening of September 19, 2026, the Sichuan University Science Fiction Association hosted the theme salon "Give Civilization to Years — Commemorating the 20th Anniversary of The Three-Body Problem". Faculty guests Jiang Zhenyu, Hu Yirong, and Zhao Hui joined students to look back on twenty years of reading memories and philosophical reflections on *The Three-Body Problem*.

Twenty years is enough time for a novel to transform from a "new release" into the collective reading memory of an entire generation. As the characters, propositions, and imaginations within *The Three-Body Problem* continue to be discussed, dialogues surrounding the universe, civilization, technology, and humanity continue to evolve across differing cultural and historical contexts.

## First Encounter with Three-Body | Reliving Reading Memories

The event kicked off in a relaxed atmosphere with "First Impressions of Three-Body". The three guest teachers each shared their personal journeys of discovering *The Three-Body Problem* and the characters that moved them most: Ye Wenjie, Luo Ji's "dream muse", and Listener 1379.

![Guest teachers sharing their stories with The Three-Body Problem](/images/events/three-body-20th/image-02.jpg)

<p align="center" class="no-indent" style="color: #888; font-size: 0.9em; margin-top: -0.5rem; margin-bottom: 1.5rem;">
  <sub>Guest teachers sharing their stories with The Three-Body Problem</sub>
</p>

The salon also unveiled survey results collected from students beforehand—covering the age they first read *The Three-Body Problem*, their favorite installments in the trilogy, and their most cherished characters, allowing everyone to see their shared resonance with the teachers.

## Revisiting Civilization | Looking Back Across Twenty Years

Next, the three teachers engaged in deep dialogues surrounding the 20th anniversary of *The Three-Body Problem*. Drawing from their academic disciplines and reading experiences, they explored interpretations of "civilization", the significance of science fiction to human civilization, and reflections on the contemporary development of Chinese civilization.

The host also posed a question regarding which sci-fi concepts from *The Three-Body Problem* they most wished to see become reality, to which each teacher provided insightful reflections.

## Clashing Thoughts | Dialogue on Technology and the Humanities

During the open Q&A session, students posed thought-provoking questions on topics such as understanding AI training dynamics—drawing from both human-curated and AI-generated materials—and the enduring importance of humanism in science fiction literature. The guest teachers addressed each inquiry from the vantage points of their research domains and personal philosophies.

The event concluded with a raffle featuring three copies of *Science Fiction World* magazines, five printed autograph posters of Liu Cixin, and one book personally signed by Liu Cixin, culminating in a group photo.

The evening's discussion began with *The Three-Body Problem* and radiated outward into broader questions of literature, technology, and reality.
